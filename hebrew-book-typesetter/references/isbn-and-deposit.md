# ISBN, imprint page and legal deposit

## ISBN (מסת"ב)

- Issuer: the Israel Center for Libraries (המרכז לספריות וספרות בישראל), https://icl.org.il/isbn.
  The National Library does NOT issue ISBNs.
- Price list on the ICL site (check before ordering): one ISBN 160 NIS without a barcode,
  195 NIS with a barcode.
- Standard allocation: within 5 working days of the order and payment.
- At most 4 single numbers per buyer, then blocks of 10, 100 or 1,000 only.
- Israeli numbers start 978-965.
- Print the ISBN on the back of the title page (the publisher's page) and on the back cover,
  with the barcode on the back cover.
- A new format (softcover, hardcover) or a revised edition needs a new ISBN.
- An ISBN does not protect copyright, and it is not required by law.

## Imprint page (section 3 of the Books Law)

Every book published in Israel states, at its start or end:
- the year of publication and the year of printing,
- the name and address of the printer and of the publisher (a self-publishing author is the publisher),
- that it was published in Israel.

## Legal deposit

Statute: the Books Law (Duty of Delivery and Specification of Particulars), 5761-2000, and the
Books Regulations.

- Applies to a book produced in Israel and offered to the public in 50 or more copies, paid or free.
- 2 complete copies, free, to the National Library, within 30 days of publication.
- The duty falls on the first with an Israeli address of: the publisher or producer, the
  printer, the copyright owner.
- Exempt: books for a limited audience, such as family members, guests at a private event,
  or the staff or students of an educational institution (Books Regulations). This covers most
  family memoirs.
- A re-publication with any change in content or form must be deposited again.
- Procedure: the National Library's online deposit form issues a deposit number; then the
  copies are brought or mailed. https://www.nli.org.il/he/at-your-service/who-we-are/depositing
- Electronic editions: under the Books Regulations they are delivered in the electronic
  format they are distributed in, through a dedicated part of the National Library's website,
  with any password or usage block removed.
- Other copies: one copy of a book needed for the Knesset's work goes to the Knesset Library,
  which pays for it; one copy of a paper book goes to the State Archives, but only if it asks.
- Penalty: not depositing, or not printing the section 3 details, is an offence punishable by
  a fine under the Penal Law (section 8 of the Books Law).
- Explain these rules and let the user apply them to their own print run.
