# Domain Coverage Checklist: Shabbat- and Chag-Aware Israeli Scheduling

Scope contract for `shabbat-aware-scheduler`. Each row is a distinct dimension along which an
Israeli scheduling answer can be wrong. A row is "covered" only when the skill states the rule
AND the bundled script implements it. Rows are deliberately split per varying dimension: one
row per thing that varies independently, because each one has its own failure mode.

Legend for the verdict column when auditing: OK / PARTIAL / MISSING.

---

## Must cover (core)

Failing any of these produces a wrong scheduling decision: a meeting or deploy landing inside a
prohibited window, a wrong onset time, or a missed national closure. | # | Item | The rule that must be stated | Authoritative source | |---|------|------------------------------|----------------------| | M1 | Shabbat onset by city minhag | Candle lighting is a per-city offset before sunset: **Jerusalem 40 min**, **Haifa and Zikhron Ya'akov 30 min**, **18 min** everywhere else in Israel. A single global `b=` value is wrong by 10-22 minutes for two of the three groups. Prefer passing `geonameid` so Hebcal applies the city's own minhag. | Hebcal candle-lighting documentation, https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api (`b=` parameter and per-city defaults) | | M2 | Shabbat end (havdalah) convention | Three mutually incompatible conventions must be chosen explicitly, never mixed: **tzeit hakochavim at sun 8.5 deg below the horizon** (Hebcal `M=on`, which in Israel lands ~37 min after sunset in late summer and ~40 min in midwinter), **fixed minutes after sunset** (`m=42`, `m=50`), and **Rabbeinu Tam 72 minutes** (`m=72`). `m=N` is a minute count, not a star count. | Hebcal REST API `M` / `m` parameters, https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api ; verify against https://www.hebcal.com/zmanim?cfg=json&latitude=LAT&longitude=LON&tzid=Asia/Jerusalem&date=YYYY-MM-DD (`sunset`, `tzeit42min`, `tzeit72min`) | | M3 | Onset is a MOMENT, not a date | Shabbat and every Yom Tov begin at candle lighting the **evening before** the calendar date and end at nightfall. Any check that fires at a specific instant (cron, deploy, notification, alert page) must compare `now` against the live onset-to-end window; a weekday check alone fires during Shabbat every Friday evening. | Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951, s.7(a): the weekly rest is **at least 36 consecutive hours** and for a Jewish worker includes Shabbat,  / Ministry of Labour, | | M4 | The same moment-window applies to Yom Tov and its erev | Erev Pesach 20:30, erev Yom Kippur 19:30 and Hoshana Raba evening are already inside the chag. A Shabbat-only live-window check green-lights all of them. Every erev-chag date needs its own candle-lighting time, not just Fridays. | Hebcal returns `candles` items for erev chag as well as erev Shabbat: https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api | | M5 | Yom Tov vs chol ha-moed | Melacha-prohibited Yom Tov days are a hard closure. Chol ha-moed (Pesach II-VI, Sukkot II-VII) is a **legal workday** in Israel with thin staffing, schools closed and government offices on reduced hours. Hebcal marks only Yom Tov with `yomtov: true`. | Hebcal `yomtov` flag; Israeli school-vacation calendar, Ministry of Education, | | M6 | One-day Israel vs two-day diaspora | Israel keeps one day of Yom Tov for Pesach I/VII, Shavuot, Sukkot I and Shmini Atzeret (Simchat Torah merged into it); Rosh Hashana is two days in both. Requesting the diaspora calendar blocks workdays that are chol ha-moed in Israel. Always pass `i=on`. | Hebcal `i=on` Israeli schedule flag, https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api | | M7 | National closures that are NOT Yom Tov: Yom HaAtzma'ut | 5 Iyyar. A statutory national holiday; government, banks, schools and most businesses closed. Hebcal returns it with `yomtov: false`, so a `yomtov`-only filter reports it as an ordinary workday. | Independence Day Law 5709-1949 (חוק יום העצמאות), | | M8 | National closures: Yom HaZikaron | 4 Iyyar, immediately preceding Yom HaAtzma'ut. Opens with the 20:00 siren the **evening before**; places of entertainment are closed by law from that evening. Restricted commerce all day. `yomtov: false`. | Memorial Day Law 5723-1963 (חוק יום הזיכרון לחללי מערכות ישראל), | | M9 | National closures: Yom HaShoah | 27 Nisan. A workday, but entertainment venues are closed by law from the preceding evening and there is a 10:00 national siren. No launches, parties or celebratory events. | Martyrs' and Heroes' Remembrance Day Law 5719-1959, | | M10 | Erev Yom Kippur and the Yom Kippur shutdown | Erev Yom Kippur is a half day nationally; from candle lighting the country stops completely for ~25 hours: no flights, no broadcast, no public transport, roads empty. Treat as a stricter Shabbat. Erev YK carries `yomtov: false`. | Hebcal Israeli feed; Israel Airports Authority Yom Kippur closure notices, | | M11 | Tisha B'Av | 9 Av (10 Av when 9 Av falls on Shabbat). A major fast; public sector and many private employers run a half day or close, entertainment venues closed by law. `yomtov: false`. | Hebcal Israeli feed; Prohibition of Opening Entertainment Venues on Tisha B'Av Law 5758-1997 | | M12 | The other fast days | **Tzom Gedaliah (3 Tishrei), Asara B'Tevet (10 Tevet), Ta'anit Esther (13 Adar / Adar II), Tzom Tammuz (17 Tammuz), Ta'anit Bechorot (erev Pesach)** are dawn-to-nightfall fasts: reduced-capacity days, no catered meetings, early finish. **Hebcal omits them entirely unless `mf=on` is passed** - `maj`/`min`/`mod` do not return them. | Hebcal `mf` (minor fasts) request flag, https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api | | M13 | City-dependent observance: Shushan Purim | 15 Adar. A full holiday in Jerusalem and other historically walled cities, an ordinary workday nationally. The only Israeli observance whose status depends on the **city**, not the date. A national scheduler must not block it; a Jerusalem scheduler must not ignore it. | Mishnah Megillah 1:1; Hebcal `Shushan Purim` entry, category `minor`, | | M14 | Erev chag half days | Erev Rosh Hashana, Erev Yom Kippur, Erev Pesach, Erev Sukkot, Erev Shavuot, Erev Shmini Atzeret (Hoshana Raba) and Erev Tish'a B'Av are morning-only nationally; the public sector closes around 12:00-13:00. Collective agreements make erev chag a shortened paid day. | General collective agreement on shortened working days on erev chag; Hebcal `Erev *` entries | | M15 | The Israeli work week is Sunday-Thursday | Sunday is the **first** business day, Saturday is the weekly rest day. A Monday-Friday assumption misplaces both ends of every week. | Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951, s.7; Bank of Israel banking-hours directives, | | M16 | Friday is a half day, and for some sectors not a day at all | Friday closes early, and the cutoff is **seasonal, not fixed**: it tracks candle lighting, so a winter Friday can end by 13:00 while a June Friday runs later. Banks, government offices and much of hi-tech do not open Friday at all. Never hardcode a 17:00 or even a 13:00 Friday cutoff without checking that Friday's candle-lighting time. | Bank of Israel banking hours; Hebcal per-Friday `candles` time | | M17 | Israeli DST is its own rule | DST starts at 02:00 on the **Friday before the last Sunday of March** and ends at 02:00 on the **last Sunday of October** - matching neither the US nor the EU. Both shifts move Friday candle lighting by an hour in wall-clock terms; the October end is sharper because Shabbat suddenly begins an hour earlier. Always resolve times through the `Asia/Jerusalem` tz database, never a fixed UTC offset. | Time Determination Law 5752-1992 as amended 2013 (חוק קביעת הזמן),  ; IANA tzdata `Asia/Jerusalem` | | M18 | Hebrew leap years and Adar II | 7 of every 19 years carry a second Adar. In a leap year **Purim and Ta'anit Esther move to Adar II** (14/13 Adar II) while Purim Katan sits on 14 Adar I, and a Hebrew-date anniversary computed as "14 Adar" silently lands a month early. Never do Hebrew-date arithmetic on the Gregorian calendar. | Hebcal Hebrew-date converter, https://www.hebcal.com/converter?cfg=json&gy=YEAR&gm=MONTH&gd=DAY&g2h=1 ; Hebcal leap-year documentation, | | M19 | Yom HaAtzma'ut / Yom HaZikaron postponement (nidcheh) | The pair is moved so Yom HaZikaron never abuts Shabbat: if 5 Iyyar falls on **Friday or Shabbat** the pair is brought **forward** (to Wed/Thu), and if 5 Iyyar falls on **Monday** the pair is **postponed** to Tue/Wed so Yom HaZikaron does not fall on Sunday. Never compute 5 Iyyar directly; read the observed date from a calendar source that applies the rule. | Independence Day Law 5709-1949 as amended (postponement clause); Hebcal, which applies the rule, | | M20 | Fail closed, never fail open | When the calendar source is unreachable, rate-limited or returns nothing, the answer must be "do not schedule", not "ordinary business day". An empty holiday cache is the single highest-frequency path to booking a meeting on Yom Kippur. | Operational rule; supported by Hebcal's documented 90-requests-per-10-seconds limit returning HTTP 429, https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api |

## Should cover (advanced)

Important best practices and recurring edge cases. Missing these degrades quality without
necessarily producing an illegal or impossible slot. | # | Item | The rule | Authoritative source | |---|------|----------|----------------------| | S1 | Non-Jewish statutory rest days | A non-Jewish employee in Israel may elect their own weekly rest day (Friday for Muslims, Sunday for Christians) and is entitled to their own festivals as holidays: Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Christmas/Easter (Western and Orthodox dates differ), the Druze Nabi Shu'ayb. A mixed Israeli team is not covered by the Hebrew calendar alone. | Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951, s.7(b)-(c); Ministry of Interior annual holiday order listing recognised non-Jewish festivals, | | S2 | Election day is a paid national day off | A Knesset election day is a statutory paid holiday; transport is free, most workplaces and schools close. It appears in no Hebrew-calendar API and must come from the Central Elections Committee. | Knesset Elections Law [Consolidated Version] 5729-1969, s.136| | S3 | Multi-day compound closures | Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat creates 3-day (and in the diaspora 4-day) unbroken no-work spans - e.g. Rosh Hashana falling Sat-Sun, or a Sunday Yom Tov after Shabbat. Detect the span, do not classify each date in isolation. | Hebcal Israeli feed for the target year | | S4 | Mourning periods that block celebratory events | Sefirat HaOmer (Pesach to Shavuot) restricts weddings and concerts for most observers; the **Three Weeks** (17 Tammuz to 9 Av) and more strictly the **Nine Days** (1-9 Av) rule out launch parties, offsites and celebratory events even though every one of those days is a legal workday. | Shulchan Aruch OC 493, 551; Hebcal calendar entries for the period | | S5 | Seasonal Friday cutoff must be computed, not tabulated | A per-month approximation table drifts and breaks across DST boundaries. Derive Friday's cutoff from that specific Friday's candle-lighting time minus a travel buffer. | Hebcal `/shabbat` per-week response | | S6 | Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, Tu BiShvat, Lag BaOmer | Ordinary workdays. Do **not** block them. Purim is a partial exception: schools closed, many workplaces run a short or costume day, and municipal services are reduced. | Hebcal categories `minor` / `roshchodesh` | | S7 | School-vacation windows drive parent availability | Chol ha-moed Sukkot and Pesach, the Chanukah week, and the whole of July-August are school holidays: early finishes and mass leave regardless of workplace status. | Ministry of Education school-year calendar, | | S8 | Location matters beyond minhag | Sunset and therefore both Shabbat boundaries differ measurably between Eilat, Tel Aviv and Metula. Resolve times per city, not once for the country. | Hebcal `geonameid` / lat-lon parameters | | S9 | Offline and rate-limit fallback | Cache a full year of holidays and refresh at Rosh Hashana; for airgapped or CI use, prefer `@hebcal/core` (JS), `hebcal-go`, or `pyluach` plus a zmanim source, rather than an unguarded HTTP call. |  ;| | S10 | Boundary-safe date windows | Any range search that can cross 31 December must load holiday data for **both** Gregorian years; a single-year cache silently classifies January as holiday-free. | Hebcal `year` parameter semantics | | S11 | Rounding and buffer policy | Do not schedule up to the minute of candle lighting. State an explicit buffer (commonly 1-2 hours before onset for meetings, and a longer one for deploys so a rollback fits before the window). | Operational rule | | S12 | Deploy-specific stringency | A deploy must be able to roll back **and be monitored**, so the last safe deploy is bounded by staff availability, not by the onset itself. Mid-week mornings; never Thursday afternoon, Friday, or erev chag. | Operational rule | | S13 | Test fixtures pinned to known-hard dates | Regression-test against dates that have historically broken schedulers: a Saturday query, an erev-chag evening, Shushan Purim, a leap-year Purim, both DST switch weekends, and a nidcheh Yom HaAtzma'ut year. | Operational rule |

## Out of scope (explicit) | Item | Why | |------|-----| | Halachic rulings (psak) on whether a specific activity is permitted | Requires a rabbi. The skill supplies times and closures, not permissibility. | | Which havdalah / candle-lighting minhag a user "should" follow | A communal and personal decision; the skill exposes the options and their numeric consequences. | | Diaspora two-day Yom Tov scheduling | Explicitly excluded by the skill description; the whole calendar model is `i=on` Israeli observance. | | Full zmanim beyond Shabbat/chag boundaries (alot hashachar, sof zman kriat shema, plag hamincha, mincha ketana) | Prayer-time computation is a separate domain; the skill only needs onset and end. | | Eruv status, local municipal bylaws, per-neighbourhood chumrot | Not derivable from any calendar API. | | Hebrew-date lifecycle arithmetic (yahrzeit, bar mitzvah dates) | Adjacent domain; use a Hebrew-date library directly. | | Non-Israeli Jewish community calendars and their local candle-lighting customs | Out of the localization scope of an Israel-specific skill. | | Payroll consequences of erev chag / holiday work (premium rates, holiday pay) | Employment-law and payroll domain, not scheduling. | | Non-Jewish religious calendar computation (Hijri, Orthodox Julian dates) | The skill should *flag* the need (S1) and defer to a dedicated source. |

## Note on citations

Sources in this file are cited by NAME where a stable, verified URL was not confirmed in this
cycle. Statute names are the load-bearing citation; an unverified gov.il landing-page URL is not,
and several gov.il paths return a 200 SPA shell for a page that does not exist. Only URLs that are
also present in `evidence.json`, and therefore checked by `verify-evidence.py --check-sources`,
appear here as links.
