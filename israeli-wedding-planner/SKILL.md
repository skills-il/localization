# Israeli Wedding Planner

## Before You Use Any Price in This Skill

Every price here is an indicative market range, not a quote. Israeli wedding pricing varies enormously by region, season, day of the week and guest count, and no official body publishes price tables for wedding vendors. Use these bands to sanity-check a quote you already hold or to sketch a first draft, nothing more, and get written quotes from at least three vendors per category before you commit. All amounts are in new Israeli shekels unless stated otherwise. Israeli VAT has been 18% since 1 January 2025, and venues are inconsistent about whether a per-plate quote is before or after VAT. Ask every vendor which one they quoted, in writing. On a 200-plate wedding the difference is roughly the whole photography budget.

## Wedding Budget Breakdown

A standard mid-range Israeli wedding in 2026 typically lands somewhere around **120,000-180,000**, and high-end center-Israel venues with 400-650 guests typically run **250,000 and up**. In the periphery the range drops to roughly 100,000-150,000. These are indicative bands, not quotes. The single largest expense is the venue and catering.

The table below is an indicative allocation, not a rule. Get quotes before you fix any line.

|----------|-------------------|-------------|-------|
| Venue + catering | 70,000-130,000 | roughly 55-65 | Per-plate x guests. Biggest variable |
| Photography + video | 7,000-18,000 | roughly 8-12 | Still + video package (basic from ~5K, full premium 10-15K) |
| Music (DJ or band) | 4,000-15,000 | roughly 4-8 | Plus the ACUM license fee (see below) |
| Wedding dress | 3,000-15,000 | roughly 3-8 | Purchase, rental, or custom |
| Groom's suit | 1,500-5,000 | roughly 1-3 | Purchase or rental |

### Dates You Cannot Book

Price is the second question. Availability under Jewish law and Israeli statute is the first, and a date that fails here cannot be bought at any price.

| Date | Status | Note |
|------|--------|------|
| Shabbat | No weddings | A chuppah is not held on Shabbat |
| Chol HaMoed (Pesach, Sukkot) | No weddings | "אין נושאין נשים במועד". Betrothal without a feast is permitted, the wedding itself is not |
| Yom Tov and public fast days | Confirm before booking | Treat as unavailable and check the specific date with your mesader kiddushin |
| Three Weeks | Depends on your edah | See below |
| Yom HaZikaron and Yom HaShoah | Venues closed by law | Public entertainment may not be held; see below |

**The Three Weeks are not the same for everyone.** Sephardi practice (Shulchan Aruch OC 551:2) bars weddings only from Rosh Chodesh Av until the fast of 9 Av. The Ashkenazi custom, recorded by the Rema there, is the stringent one running from 17 Tammuz through 9 Av. A Sephardi couple therefore has roughly two extra weeks in one of the cheapest parts of the calendar, and telling them the whole three weeks is closed hands back real money for no reason. Confirm with your mesader kiddushin.

**The start of the Omer restriction is not uniform either.** The Shulchan Aruch bars weddings from Pesach until Lag BaOmer (OC 493:1). The Rema records communities whose mourning period instead begins at Rosh Chodesh Iyar (OC 493:3, said there about haircuts). Which custom governs your date is a question for your mesader kiddushin, not an assumption. Ask before you write April off.

**Two statutory dead dates that have nothing to do with halacha.** On Yom HaZikaron and on Yom HaShoah, Israeli law prohibits public entertainment (עינוגים ציבוריים), with a fine for breach, and each day runs from sunset to nightfall the next day. Read the statutes precisely: they bar holding a public-entertainment event, they do not order every building shut. The practical effect is the same, no hall will host you. Both fall inside the Omer period, so the Omer discussion above does not cover them.

**If either partner was previously married**, a divorced or widowed woman waits 90 days before remarrying (Shulchan Aruch EH 13:1, the הבחנה period), counted excluding the day of the divorce or death and the day of the new betrothal. This applies even where pregnancy is not possible. Which event starts the count is disputed, so confirm the exact date with the marriage-registration bureau before booking, and do not treat a figure you read online as the date the bureau will accept.

## Rabbinate Registration (Tik Nisuin)

### Timeline

Open a marriage file at your local Religious Council **as early as possible, and no later than 45 days before the wedding**. That 45-day floor is the Ministry of Religious Services' own rule, and it is a hard cut-off, not a guideline.

ITIM, which represents couples through this process, states a file can be opened **up to three months ahead**, so three months out is the earliest practical point and about when most couples go. Documents and witness testimony carry their own validity windows, so starting earlier mainly means redoing paperwork. The binding constraint is the 45-day floor, not any ceiling.

Since registration areas were opened up, you may register at **any marriage-registration bureau you choose, regardless of where you live**. Some bureaus let you begin the process online through the Shirat HaYam system (https://shirathayam.m-datit.org.il/rishum), but this only starts the registration, both partners must still appear in person together.

### Required Documents

| Document | Who | Notes |
|----------|-----|-------|
| Teudat Zehut (ID) | Both | Original, not copy |
| Passport photo | One per partner | The Ministry asks for one passport photo of each partner, and the marriage bureau can photograph you on the spot if you arrive without one |
| Parents' Ketuba | Both | Original or certified copy |
| Teudat Ravakut | Both, when the bureau asks for it | Certificate of single status. Registration areas are now open, so you may register at any bureau; ask the bureau you picked whether it wants this certificate or will take witness testimony instead |
| Registration fee | Both | 844 from 1 January 2026, per the schedule to the Jewish Religious Services (Fees and Services) Regulations as published in Reshumot (Kovetz HaTakanot 12128). Index-linked and re-published each year, so confirm before paying. A 40% discount applies to eligible groups |
| Kosher certificate of venue | Couple | Practice varies by council, confirm with the one you register at (see Gotcha 5) |
| Birth certificate | Olim/converts | Required for immigrants |
| Conversion documents | Converts | Original papers + rabbi's letter |

### Process Steps

1. **Gather documents** -- collect all items above
2. **Visit local Religious Council** -- both bride and groom must appear together
3. **Interview** -- the Rabbinate official verifies Jewish status and eligibility
4. **Schedule chuppah** -- the Rabbinate assigns a mesader kiddushin (officiating rabbi) or you request your own
5. **Bride's mikveh visit** -- required before the wedding, typically 1-7 days before
6. **Wedding day** -- mesader kiddushin conducts chuppah, signs ketuba, witnesses sign

### Berur Yahadut (Jewish-Status Inquiry)

If neither partner's parents hold an Israeli Rabbinate marriage certificate or ketuba, the bureau may open a berur yahadut. Treat it as a separate track, not a document you bring. You open a file at the regional rabbinical court nearest your home, an investigator meets you and forms recommendations, and a dayan rules at a hearing. It costs nothing, runs through a national intake centre with teams by country of origin, and a decision can be appealed to the Beit Din HaGadol within 30 days.

Two things decide whether this costs you your date. First, no service standard is published, so nobody can honestly tell you how long it takes; do not plan against a figure you found online. The Ministry's own advice is to do it at any stage of life rather than near the wedding, and as early as possible, because documents get lost, archives get harder, and testimony from elderly relatives is a wasting asset. Second, if a first-degree relative already married in Israel through a berur, you may be exempt entirely, so ask before you start. The document list, the exemption and ITIM's role are in `references/legal-and-contract-risks.md`.

### Common Rabbinate Pitfalls

- Kohanim cannot marry divorced women or converts (must verify before planning)
- If parents married abroad, additional documentation proving Jewish status may be required
- Some Religious Councils have long wait times -- book early
- The Rabbinate-approved mesader kiddushin is free; choosing your own rabbi is optional and additional cost

### Venue Licensing and Safety

An event hall is a licensable business: item 7.9 of the Business Licensing Order (Licensable Businesses), 2013, "a hall or garden for celebrations and events", inside group 7 (public entertainment). Section 4 of the Business Licensing Law, 1968 makes operating without a licence an offence, and compliance with the fire laws is a licensing purpose for item 7.9 with no self-declaration shortcut, so a licensed hall has actually been through the fire authority.

Your check is concrete. Regulation 41 of the Business Licensing (General Provisions) Regulations, 2001 requires the licence to be displayed at the business in a visible place, so ask to see it on the venue tour. If it is not up, ask the local authority's business-licensing department, which is the licensing authority under section 5(a)(1). Separately, ask the hall to put its maximum occupancy in writing and compare it against your guest list before you sign.

This is not paperwork pedantry. A mayor may order an unlicensed business shut where it creates a real danger to the public (section 20), and neither that nor a court closure on conviction gives you your date back. There is no venue-specific statutory remedy for a couple whose hall is closed. See `references/legal-and-contract-risks.md`.

### ACUM (Israeli Composers and Authors Society)

Any public performance of music at a wedding requires an **ACUM license**. The family-event tariff (T008) is a fixed amount per event covering a DJ, band, live singer, background music, or any combination. Under the 2026 tariff it is **395.30 including VAT** per single event. ACUM re-publishes the tariff annually, so confirm the current figure on the ACUM licensing site before paying. The license must be paid and reported at least 72 hours before the event at acum.org.il. This covers royalties to songwriters and composers. Either the DJ or the couple pays it, clarify in the contract. Without it, the DJ risks a fine.

## If the Wedding Cannot Go Ahead

Gathering limits and reserve call-ups have cancelled Israeli weddings repeatedly. This is a contract term you negotiate, not an act of God you hope about.

**What the consumer regulations give you, and when they stop.** Hosting, travel and leisure services performed in Israel are item (8) of the schedule to the Consumer Protection (Cancellation of a Transaction) Regulations, 2010; only services performed entirely outside Israel are excluded, under regulation 6(a)(12). But the window in regulation 2(3) is short and cumulative: within 14 days of making the agreement, and at least 14 non-rest days before the service begins. Inside it the cancellation fee is capped at the lower of 5 percent or 100 shekels. A hall booked a year ahead and cancelled months later is long past that, and the deposit, the cancellation ladder and any right to postpone are then purely contractual. Never tell a couple they have a 5 percent right once the window has closed.

**Force majeure is a shield, not a refund.** Section 18(a) of the Contracts (Remedies for Breach of Contract) Law, 1970 only removes enforcement and damages, and only where the circumstances were unforeseeable and unpreventable. Money comes back, if at all, through section 18(b), which is a discretion the court exercises where it seems just.

**So write the clause in advance.** Ask for a postponement right defined separately from cancellation, triggered by objectively verifiable events (a declared special home-front situation, a Home Front Command instruction restricting gatherings in your instruction zone, a closure order, a reserve call-up), with the deposit transferring in full to a new date inside a stated window. Event cover including cancellation is sold in Israel, but read the exclusions clause and ask the agent in writing whether a home-front gathering restriction is a covered trigger. That clause decides the claim. Detail is in `references/legal-and-contract-risks.md`.

## Civil Marriage Outside the Rabbinate

The Rabbinate is the only path to a religious Jewish wedding registered inside Israel. Couples who cannot or will not marry through it (interfaith, same-sex, divorced women marrying Kohanim, secular couples objecting on principle) have two main routes whose results are recognized by the Population Authority and registered on the teudat zehut:

| Route | Typical cost | Process | Recognition |
|-------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| Cyprus civil marriage | roughly 3,000-7,000 all-in for travel, ceremony and apostille (indicative) | 1-2 day trip, marriage at Cypriot municipality, apostille on certificate, return to Israel, register at Misrad HaPnim with notarized Hebrew translation | Full civil recognition |
| Utah online marriage (Zoom) | roughly 1,900-3,000 (indicative) | Ceremony performed by an officiant in Utah over Zoom from anywhere, apostilled certificate, register at Misrad HaPnim | An administrative court ordered the registrar to register such a marriage (Adm. Pet. (Center) 12316-03-21), and the Supreme Court dismissed the appeal and upheld that ruling. Registered in the population registry |

**What registration does and does not mean.** Getting the marriage into the population registry is not a ruling that it is valid for every purpose. The entry records what you presented; it does not adjudicate personal status. Utah's 2026 amendment now requires the officiant to warn you out loud that an online marriage may be invalid where you live, which is the same point from the other side. For most practical Israeli purposes (status on the teudat zehut, benefits, joint filing) registration is what you need.

**Registration is not status for a foreign spouse.** If one partner is not an Israeli citizen, getting the marriage into the registry does not give that partner the right to live here. Procedure 5.2.0008 of the Population and Immigration Authority says in terms that updating personal status to "married" carries no determination about the genuineness of the relationship or the arranging of status. The graduated process still applies: up to six months on a B/1 licence while the file is checked, which does not count toward the process, then A/5 temporary residency, first for one year and up to four years in total, with an interview testing genuineness of the relationship, a joint centre of life, and absence of a security or criminal impediment. Three months before the four years end both partners appear in person and choose citizenship or permanent residency. No published policy treats an online marriage differently from a Cyprus one for status, so the certificate gets you registered and shortcuts nothing. For many couples this, not the ceremony, is the real reason they took the civil route. See `references/legal-and-contract-risks.md`.

**Practical notes:**
- Cypriot municipalities differ in what they accept, so confirm with the specific municipality before you fly whether it wants one combined file or separate documentation per partner. At Misrad HaPnim, ask a counter clerk to print a fresh תמצית רישום rather than using the self-service kiosk, which some offices will not accept.
- Israel recognizes the marriage but does not change religious status. The Rabbinate considers civil-marriage couples as un-married for halachic purposes (relevant for future divorce, which goes through Rabbinical Court regardless).
- Utah online ceremonies are faster and cheaper than Cyprus and avoid travel entirely, but the legal landscape can shift on the Utah side as well as the Israeli one. Utah amended its marriage statute in 2026 (S.B. 257, amending Utah Code 81-2-302), and your officiant will now read a warning aloud before the ceremony. That is the law working as intended, not a problem with your ceremony. What the amendment does, and how close the route came to being closed entirely, is in `references/legal-and-contract-risks.md`. Verify the current position before booking.

### 1 Month Before (also: the prenup deadline)

A spousal property agreement (הסכם ממון) signed **before** the wedding can be authenticated by a notary, and one made before or at the time of the marriage can alternatively be authenticated by the marriage registrar. Once you are married, the same agreement needs approval from the Family Court or the religious court instead. This is the one item on the whole timeline that gets structurally harder the day after the chuppah, so decide before, not after. Signing one is optional, missing the cheap route by a day is not a decision anyone makes on purpose.

There is a second, separate pre-chuppah document couples conflate with it: a standard prenuptial agreement aimed at preventing get-refusal. It does not undertake to give or receive a get, since that would undermine the voluntariness the act requires; instead it imposes duties such as couples counselling, appearing at the rabbinical court, and paying elevated maintenance for each period of delay. Tzohar, ITIM with Yad La'isha, and Kolech each publish a free form, with authentication differing by form (Kolech's requires a notary, ITIM's is signed before a lawyer). All recommend signing before the chuppah, though unlike the property agreement it can also be signed afterwards, so do not tell a couple the door closes at the chuppah. No Israeli law makes it mandatory; the bills that would have done so were never enacted.

## Gotchas

### 1. ACUM Fee Surprise
Agents often forget the ACUM license fee when calculating music costs. It is mandatory for any public music performance at a wedding, including DJ, band, live singer, or background playlist. Family-event tariff: ACUM tariff T008, 395.30 including VAT per event under the 2026 tariff (re-published annually, confirm on acum.org.il). Must be paid at least 72 hours before the event via acum.org.il. Include it in the budget and clarify in the DJ contract who pays.

### 2. Rabbinate Deadline Window
The binding rule is a **floor, not a window**: the file must be opened no later than 45 days before the wedding. Do not tell a user they have until 21 days, and do not tell them a file opened more than 90 days out will be rejected. Both are wrong, and the first can cost a couple their date. The figure in the guidance is three months, not 90 days, and it is the softer constraint.

### 3. Sefirat HaOmer and Three Weeks Restrictions
Agents commonly schedule weddings during Sefirat HaOmer or the Three Weeks, when weddings are not held. Check the Hebrew calendar, and which edah's custom applies, before suggesting dates.

### 4. Per-Plate Does Not Include Everything
The per-plate price typically covers food and basic hall rental, but NOT DJ setup area rental, valet parking, design and flowers, external catering surcharges, or Shabbat early-closure surcharges. Ask what is and isn't included.

### 5. Kashrut Certificate Mismatch
Religious Councils generally expect the venue to hold Rabbinate kashrut, and a venue carrying only a private hechsher such as a Badatz can raise a question. Practice varies between councils and is not published as a uniform national rule, so confirm with the council you register at rather than assuming. A private hechsher is usually held in addition to Rabbinate kashrut rather than instead of it, so most venues are fine.

## Troubleshooting

### Error: "Rabbinate rejected our application"
Cause: Missing documents (most commonly parents' ketuba or teudat ravakut)
Solution: Contact the specific Religious Council to ask which document is missing. If parents' ketuba is lost, request a copy from the Rabbinate where parents married.

### Error: "Venue price increased after booking"
Cause: The contract may allow adjustments for guest-count changes, menu upgrades or index linkage
Solution: Read the original contract first, because by this point the answer is almost always contractual. The statutory cancellation right under the Consumer Protection (Cancellation of a Transaction) Regulations runs out 14 days after the agreement was made, so it will not help a booking made months ago, and the 5 percent / 100 shekel cap goes with it. What remains is the contract itself, general contract law, and the Standard Contracts Law where a term is unduly disadvantageous. The Consumer Protection Authority is still the address for a complaint about conduct, but do not promise the couple a statutory price freeze.
