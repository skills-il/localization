# Legal and Contract Risks: Venue Licensing, Status Inquiry, Cancellation, Prenups, Foreign Spouse

Companion detail for `SKILL.md`. Everything here is a signpost: what the thing is, who
decides it, roughly how long it takes, and who to see. Nothing here is legal advice, and
nothing here is a conclusion about any particular couple's situation. Where a question turns
on your own facts, see a lawyer, a rav, or the relevant authority.

---

## 1. Venue licensing, fire approval and occupancy

### The licence

An event hall is a licensable business. The classification is **item 7.9** of the Business
Licensing Order (Licensable Businesses), 2013, inside **group 7, public entertainment,
leisure and sport**: "אולם או גן לשמחות ולאירועים, שעיקר עיסוקו הוא קיום אירועים, לרבות
הגשת משקאות משכרים לצריכה במקום ההגשה". Licence validity for that item is 15 years.

Section 4 of the Business Licensing Law, 1968 makes it an offence to operate a licensable
business without a licence, a temporary permit or an expedited permit.

### Fire approval

Section 6(a) of the Law provides that a licence is not issued without the approval of every
designated approving body (נותן אישור). For item 7.9 the marked licensing purposes include
**compliance with the fire laws** (section 1(a)(7)), and item 7.9 carries no declaration
(תצהיר) shortcut, so a licensed hall has actually been through the National Fire and Rescue
Authority rather than self-certifying.

### How a couple checks

- **Regulation 41** of the Business Licensing (General Provisions) Regulations, 2001:
  "רישיון עסק, היתר זמני או היתר מזורז יוצגו בעסק במקום נראה לעין." Ask to see it on the
  venue tour. A hall that cannot show you one has answered the question.
- The licensing authority is **the head of the local authority** or a delegate (section
  5(a)(1)), so the municipal business-licensing department is the address for a query.
- There is **no verified national online register** of business-licence status. Do not rely
  on a website claiming to be one.

### Occupancy

Ask for the occupancy figure **recorded in the hall's fire approval and licence file**, in
writing, and compare it against your guest list before signing. If the hall will not produce
it, ask the municipal business-licensing department, which holds the file. We could not verify
which instrument fixes the figure or that it is printed on the approval itself, so treat the
citation as unsettled, but the figure exists in the file and is checkable. Do not let a
salesperson's spoken number stand in for it.

### If the hall is unlicensed or gets closed

- **Section 20**: an administrative closure order (צו הפסקה מינהלי) may be issued by the
  mayor where an unlicensed business, or one operating in breach, creates "סכנה ממשית או חשש
  משמעותי לפגיעה בציבור".
- **Section 16**: on conviction the court may order the business stopped "לחלוטין או לתקופה
  שיקבע".

Neither restores your date. There is **no venue-specific statutory remedy** for a couple whose
hall is shut; you are left with ordinary contract and consumer law, which is why the
postponement clause in section 3 below matters more than any of this.

### Why this is separately regulated

The Versailles hall collapse in Jerusalem on 24 May 2001 killed and injured guests at a
wedding. The floor used a non-standard "pal-kal" system and a supporting wall had been removed
during renovations carried out without municipal or Standards Institute supervision. The
consequences were the Zeiler state commission of inquiry into the safety of buildings and
places serving the public, whose mandate expressly covered event venues under the Business
Licensing Law, and, downstream, the private review institutes (מכוני בקרה) and a maintenance
duty for existing buildings. Note precisely what this is: planning-and-building reform. We
found **no** amendment to item 7.9 or to the Business Licensing Law attributable to Versailles,
and you should not claim one.

## 2. Berur yahadut: the Jewish-status inquiry as a process

### What triggers it

Kol Zchut states the process exists "לברר ולבדוק לגבי יהדותה של אם הפונה, ולהוציא לפונה
אישור יהדות", and applies to "יהודים שנרשמו לנישואין בישראל, ואין בידיהם תעודת נישואין או
כתובה של הוריהם מהרבנות בארץ". The Ministry frames the driver plainly: about a million
citizens came from countries with no organised rabbinic marriage registration, so many need to
formally establish their Jewish status in order to marry or divorce in Israel.

### Where it goes

You open a file at the **regional rabbinical court** nearest your residence. A **מברר יהדות**
(investigator) meets you and forms recommendations for the dayanim; a **dayan** then reviews
the documents at a hearing and delivers a decision. The hearing itself averages a few minutes.
Intake runs through a dedicated centre, **5889\*, extension 3**, `yaadut@rbc.gov.il`, with
teams specialising in the CIS, Georgia, Eastern Europe, India, Ethiopia, South America and the
United States, and service available in Russian, Amharic, Spanish, English, French and others.
The official term is **מוקד בירור יהדות**; do not invent a grander unit name.

### What is asked for

Per the Ministry's service page:

- Copy of ID or passport plus the ID appendix, for each applicant.
- Ketuba or marriage certificate of the applicants' parents, and/or of relatives on the
  mother's side.
- Original birth certificates for the applicants and family: parents, grandparents, siblings,
  uncles and aunts.
- Family photographs linking the applicants to the extended family.
- Recommendation letters from recognised rabbis regarding the family's Jewishness.
- Original foreign certificates evidencing divorce, death, adoption, paternity, graduation,
  property registration, military service, a work book, a party membership book or any other
  government document.
- Miscellaneous confirmations, for example a Red Cross confirmation of wartime migration.

Kol Zchut adds that photographs with Jewish markers carry weight (mother and maternal
grandmother, a brit mila, tefillin at a bar mitzva, a synagogue, family graves bearing a magen
david), and that certificates are checked by Rabbinate staff who know the source language and
referred to forensic examination if forgery is suspected.

**No apostille or certified-translation requirement is stated** on either source. Do not assert
one.

### Witnesses

Not a formal requirement in the sources, but Kol Zchut recommends coming with your mother or
maternal grandmother so the dayan can talk to them about their childhood and communities.

### Cost, timeline and outcome

- **Free.** "הליך בירור יהדות אינו כרוך בתשלום אגרה."
- **No published service standard.** We read the full official service page and it states no
  target, no queue time and no SLA. Any "it takes X months" figure you find online is
  unattributable. The only official statement bearing on speed is causal: the sooner the
  required documents are found, the sooner the file can be moved along.
- Appeal lies to the **בית הדין הגדול לערעורים** in Jerusalem, **within 30 days** of receiving
  the decision, with reasons and supporting documents.

### The two planning levers

1. **Do it early and decoupled from the wedding.** The Ministry says the process can be done
   at any age and any stage of life, not necessarily near the wedding, and recommends doing it
   as early as possible for three stated reasons: original documents get lost, archival
   documentation gets harder, and testimony from elderly relatives can still be taken. That
   last one is a wasting asset, and it is the state's own reasoning, not advocacy framing.
2. **Check the exemption first.** If a first-degree relative already married in Israel and went
   through a berur at a rabbinical court, you may be exempt from the whole process. It also
   runs the other way: your berur may spare first-degree relatives theirs. In some countries of
   origin a community rabbi's certificate can substitute, North America being the example the
   Ministry gives.

### ITIM

ITIM works to advance berur proceedings that accord with halacha while protecting the parties'
rights, and reports roughly 4,000 such proceedings a year, overwhelmingly people from the
former Soviet Union. They also publish the list of marriage bureaus. Their public criticism is
worth knowing before you walk in: many describe the process as humiliating, and courts
sometimes prefer forensic tools such as DNA testing over halachic presumptions. We could **not**
verify that ITIM provides free individual representation for a berur; contact them and ask.

**Out of scope, deliberately:** any halachic ruling on an individual's Jewish status, mamzerut
or kohen status. This skill documents the process track and nothing else.

## 3. When the wedding cannot go ahead

### Gathering limits: the actual mechanism

Gathering restrictions are not free-floating. They rest on a declaration of a **special
home-front situation** (מצב מיוחד בעורף) by the Government under section 9C of the Civil
Defence Law, 1951, which lapses within five days unless the Foreign Affairs and Defence
Committee acts. Under section 9D, while such a state is declared, the Chief of Staff and other
listed officers may give "כל הוראה הנדרשת לשמירתם או להצלתם של חיי אדם או של רכוש". The listed
powers are introduced by "ובכלל זה" and are therefore not exhaustive, so gathering caps sit on
that general limb rather than on a named sub-paragraph. Crucially, "ההוראה תפקע" when the
declaration is cancelled or lapses: **no declared special situation, no legally binding
gathering cap.**

Home Front Command publishes the current defence policy, the instruction map, and the country's
**30 instruction zones**, and defines a gathering as "מפגש של מעל 10 אנשים יחד באותו מרחב ובאותו
זמן". Its own division of responsibility is useful in a contract argument: the "how" of running
an activity is the responsibility of the institution running it, not of Home Front Command. In
other words the hall decides how your event runs inside the limits.

### Cancellation: the correction

A widely repeated claim is that event and hosting services are excluded from the consumer
cancellation regulations. **That is backwards.** Hosting, travel, holiday and leisure services
are **item (8) of the schedule** to the Consumer Protection (Cancellation of a Transaction)
Regulations, 2010; the exclusion in **regulation 6(a)(12)** catches only services
performed **entirely outside Israel**.

What that inclusion actually buys you is narrow:

- **Regulation 2(3)** gives a cumulative double window: cancel **within 14 days of making the
  agreement**, *and* at least **14 non-rest days before the service begins**.
- **Regulation 5(a)**: inside that window the cancellation fee is the **lower of 5 percent of
  the price or 100 shekels**, plus proven card-clearing costs under 5(b). Refund within seven
  business days (regulation 4(a)).

A wedding hall is typically booked six to eighteen months ahead and cancelled months later when
something happens. By then the 14-days-from-signing window closed long ago. **Once it closes
the regulations give no statutory cancellation right and the 5 percent / 100 shekel cap does
not apply.** From that point the deposit, the cancellation ladder and any postponement right
are purely contractual, subject only to general contract law and to the Standard Contracts Law
if a term is unduly disadvantageous.

Two honest caveats: whether a wedding-hall booking is legally "hosting and leisure" within item
(8) is an interpretive question the regulation does not settle on its face and we found no
binding authority on the point; and the regulations bite only on a consumer transaction, not on
vendor-to-vendor arrangements.

### Force majeure

**Section 18(a)** of the Contracts (Remedies for Breach of Contract) Law, 1970 provides that
where a breach results from circumstances the breaching party neither knew nor should have
known of, could not have foreseen and could not have prevented, and performance in those
circumstances is impossible or fundamentally different from what was agreed, the breach is
**not a ground for enforcement or for damages**.

Read what that does and does not do. It is a **shield, not a refund right**. Restitution runs
through **section 18(b)**, under which the court **may** order each side to return what it
received, and may compensate reasonable expenses, "והכל אם נראה לבית המשפט צודק לעשות כן", a
judicial discretion, not an entitlement. A guide that tells a couple "force majeure means you
get your deposit back" is wrong.

The frequently repeated line that Israeli courts read section 18 narrowly is well-known
doctrine, but we did not verify it against a citable ruling; phrase it as the statutory
foreseeability requirement rather than as a holding. **We could not verify any wedding-hall
ruling, COVID-era or post-October 2023.** A small-claims decision circulating on law-firm blogs
is not precedent and is not cited here.

### Insurance

A product category marketed as covering **cancellation or postponement of an event** alongside
third-party liability and property damage is sold in Israel through brokers. That is as far as
the verification goes. We could **not** verify any insurer name, any product name, any premium,
or whether war and hostile action are excluded.

So the actionable instruction is a question, not a fact: read the **exclusions** clause, and ask
your agent **in writing** whether a declared special home-front situation, or a Home Front
Command instruction restricting gatherings in your instruction zone, is a **covered trigger**.
That clause, not the headline "event cancellation cover", decides the claim.

On third-party liability: we found **no** statutory or licensing requirement that a venue hold
it, so do not state one. Ask the hall for its certificate of insurance (אישור קיום ביטוח) and
ask whether the couple is added as an additional insured. Treat both as questions for the venue
and its agent.

### What to negotiate, and why it has to be in advance

Because the statutory window closes in 14 days and section 18(b) is a discretion, the clause is
the only thing you actually control. Ask for:

1. **A postponement right defined separately from cancellation**, so postponing does not fall
   into the cancellation ladder.
2. **Objectively verifiable triggers**, not the hall's discretion: a declared special home-front
   situation under section 9C; a Home Front Command instruction restricting gatherings above 10
   people in your named instruction zone; a closure order; a reserve call-up (tzav 8) of either
   partner or a first-degree relative.
3. **The deposit transferring in full to the new date**, with a cap on re-pricing and an outer
   window inside which you pick the replacement date.
4. **Who bears sub-vendor losses.** Catering, flowers and band each have their own contract and
   ladder. Ask the hall to state in writing which sub-vendors it contracts on your behalf, and
   to pass the postponement trigger through to those contracts.
5. **A written notice mechanism** with a deadline running from the day the trigger occurs, not
   from the event date.
6. The clause in the **signed contract or a signed appendix**, not a side email.

## 4. The two pre-chuppah agreements

Couples routinely conflate these. They are different documents that are often signed together.

### The spousal property agreement (הסכם ממון)

Governs money. Under section 2 of the Spousal Property Relations Law, 1973, an agreement
generally needs approval by the Family Court or the religious court, **but** one made before the
marriage or at the time of the marriage can instead be authenticated by the **marriage
registrar** (section 2(c)), and one made before the marriage can be authenticated by a
**notary** (section 2(c1)). This is the item on the timeline that genuinely gets structurally
harder the day after the chuppah.

### The get-refusal prenuptial agreement

A separate instrument aimed at preventing get-refusal. Mechanically, it **cannot** contain an
express undertaking to give or receive a get, because that would undermine the voluntariness
the act requires. Instead it imposes duties such as couples counselling, attendance at the
rabbinical court's get hearings, and payment of a substantial sum for each period of delay or
refusal, enforceable by a court.

Standard forms in circulation, all published free:

| Publisher | Form name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ארגון רבני צהר | הסכם מאהבה | Mutual elevated maintenance paid **monthly** by whichever spouse delays the separation; triggered after a counselling stage and confirmation by an agreed external arbitrator, realisable only after a waiting period of at least six months; obligations identical for both spouses |
| עתים with יד לאשה and ישראל הצעיר | הסכם למניעת סרבנות גט | Signed before a lawyer; enforceable in the Family Court or the rabbinical court |
| קולך | הסכם לכבוד הדדי | Downloadable free with the drafters' explanatory notes; **must be signed before a notary** |
| מרכז צדק לנשים | הסכם לנישואין בצדק ובהגינות | Linked from the Kolech page |

**Timing, stated accurately.** Every publisher recommends signing before the chuppah, Kolech on
the reasoning that the right moment to lay a foundation of mutual respect is before the shared
path begins. But it is **not legally unavailable afterwards**: Tzohar states expressly that the
agreement can also be signed after the wedding, and ITIM that it can be signed before or after,
before a lawyer. The claim that this agreement, like the property agreement, is lost once
married is **wrong**, and this file corrects it. The genuine pre-chuppah formality advantage
belongs to the property agreement.

**Why the ketuba is not a substitute:** the ketuba contains no provision addressing get-refusal.

**Do not overclaim adoption.** Signing is not compulsory. ITIM notes it has become increasingly
accepted among broad sections of the public and among rabbis, and separately reports that some
marriage registrars refuse to authenticate prenuptial agreements, offering help to couples who
hit that. We could **not** verify that the Rabbinate or Religious Councils raise the agreement
at registration as a matter of policy, or that named rabbanim decline to officiate without one.

**Legislation: bills only.** Several private bills would have addressed prenuptial agreements
(Knesset 19, 24 and 25). None was enacted. Do not describe any of them as law.

**Out of scope:** divorce and get-refusal proceedings themselves, and any advice on which form
suits a particular couple. That is a conversation with a lawyer.

## 5. Foreign spouse: registration is not status

This is often the actual reason a couple takes the Cyprus or Utah route, and it is the part
that takes years.

### Registration versus status

Procedure **5.2.0008** of the Population and Immigration Authority, "נוהל הטיפול במתן מעמד לבן
זוג זר הנשוי לאזרח ישראלי" (edition 16, updated 20 July 2026), says it in terms: updating the
Israeli partner's personal status to "married" carries **no determination** as to the
genuineness of the relationship or the arranging of status in Israel; that is examined under
the procedure. It also records that the right to family life is not absolute and that a foreign
spouse is **not** automatically entitled to Israeli citizenship by virtue of the marriage.

If the marriage certificate is found inadmissible under procedure 2.11.0001 (personal-status
changes), the status application is **rejected on the threshold** and the foreign spouse is
required to leave Israel within 14 days.

### The graduated process

| Stage | Licence | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| File under examination | B/1 general residence and work | Up to 6 months, and it does **not** count toward the graduated process |
| Graduated process | A/5 temporary residency | First granted for one year, up to a total of **4 years**; extensions from the second year for up to two years at a time |
| Decision point | (none) | **Three months before** the four A/5 years end, notify the bureau which status you want, citizenship or permanent residency; both spouses must attend in person |

That composition, not a figure stated in the procedure, is where "about four and a half years"
comes from. The procedure states no single total.

### The interview

Where possible an interview of the couple is held once original documentation is produced,
conducted under the interviews procedure 5.1.0013, testing three **cumulative** components:
genuineness of the couple's relationship, a joint centre of life, and absence of a security or
criminal impediment including a foreign criminal record.

Evidence required covers **at least the past year**: confirmations of services received from
government bodies including the municipality; joint photographs, letters and recommendations
from family and friends with a copy of each recommender's ID and contact details; a lease,
joint bills and a bank statement; a letter signed by both spouses explaining the relationship,
their shared life and how long they have known each other; and an affidavit.

The correct word for this is **ראיון**. The terms "מרכז השימוע" and "ועדת המשמורת" do **not**
appear in the procedure; "שימוע" appears once, and only as the hearing held before revoking a
licence.

### Online marriages

Registration of a Zoom marriage officiated from Utah with an apostilled certificate was ordered
by an administrative court and the Supreme Court dismissed the appeal. But neither 5.2.0008 nor
2.11.0001 contains the words מקוון, היוועדות, זום, יוטה or טקס, and **there is no published
policy distinguishing an online marriage from a Cyprus civil marriage for status purposes**. The
honest signpost: a Utah certificate gets you registered, it does **not** shortcut the graduated
process, and the same 5.2.0008 track applies. The residual risk sits in the certificate's
admissibility under 2.11.0001, which is what an office would test. Worth noting without
over-reading: 5.2.0008 singles out **El Salvador** certificates for special handling, routing
them to the personal-status section of procedure 5.2.0009. Utah is not named.

### Utah's 2026 amendment

Utah amended its marriage statute in 2026 by S.B. 257, amending Utah Code 81-2-302. A remote
ceremony is valid only if the issuing county publishes a warning that an online marriage
solemnized in Utah may be invalid where the couple lives, and the officiant repeats that
warning verbatim immediately before the ceremony. Worth knowing when you check the current
status: the **introduced** version of that same bill would have prohibited and voided remote
marriages outright, and that language is **not** in the enrolled text. The route survived a real
attempt to close it and could face another, so verify the current position before booking.

### Related tracks

- **5.2.0009**, "נוהל הטיפול במתן מעמד לבני זוג של ישראלים, לרבות בני אותו מין", is the
  unmarried-partner (yeduim batzibur) track. A couple in that graduated process who marry
  mid-track may convert to the married track with no further fee: a B/1 holder is upgraded to
  A/5; an existing A/5 holder completes four A/5 years counted from first issue.
- A separate procedure covers a foreign spouse of a **permanent resident** rather than a
  citizen. Do not conflate them.
- Application is made online through the Authority's service page. Failure to report a marriage
  within 30 days is an offence.

**Out of scope:** immigration advice beyond this signposting. A couple in this position should
see an immigration lawyer or an organisation working in the field.
