## How class actions over website accessibility work

- A class action may be filed only in a claim listed in the Law's Second Schedule, or where a statute expressly allows it (Class Actions Law s.3(א)). Item 9(1) of the Second Schedule lists claims under chapters ד', ה' or ה'1 of the Equal Rights Law, which is where the service-accessibility duties sit.
- Filing requires court certification (s.3(ב)). The court certifies only if, among other conditions, the claim raises material questions of fact or law common to the whole class with a reasonable chance of being decided for it, and a class action is the efficient and fair way to decide the dispute (s.8(א)).
- For a website, regulation 35א(ד)(1) means there is no violation until a fix notice was sent and not acted on within a reasonable time, at most 60 days. The Tel Aviv District Court (ת"צ 52621-07-23 בן אור נ' אביב בית השקעות, 22.1.2024, Judge Oded Ma'or) held that a prior notice is required before a class-action request at least for claims of a missing statement (35ה) and missing arrangements (34). It is a district-court decision, not a Supreme Court holding; the judgment text was read on afiklaw.com.
